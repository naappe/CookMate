Singapore-Style Black Pepper Prawns

Black pepper is the dominant character, supported by a garlic-ginger-shallot base, savoury sauces, restrained sweetness, butter and a light glossy thickening.

Main400 g prawns
Yield2–3 portions
Prep15 min
Cook8–10 min
HeatHigh → Medium

Ingredients

Prawns, cleaned400 g
Black peppercorns10 g
Cooking oil2 tbsp
Salt½ tsp
Butter1 tbsp
Garlic20 g
Ginger5 g
Shallots40 g
Dry red chillies2–3
Curry leaves1 sprig
Dark soy sauce1 tbsp
Oyster sauce1 tbsp
Tomato ketchup1 tbsp
Worcestershire1 tsp
Cornflour + water1 tsp + 2 tbsp

Shopping List

Flavour Architecture

Primary: coarsely crushed black pepper.
Aromatic base: garlic + ginger + shallots.
Savoury anchor: oyster sauce + dark soy.
Balance: ketchup + Worcestershire.
Finish: butter + cornflour slurry for gloss and coating.

Cooking Method

1. Mise en place. Clean and dry prawns. Chop aromatics. Crush pepper. Mix slurry.
2. Sear. Heat a wide pan over high heat. Quickly sear prawns and remove before overcooking.
3. Aromatics. Reduce to medium. Add oil and butter, then shallots, garlic and ginger. Add chilli and curry leaves.
4. Sauce. Add pepper, soy, oyster sauce, ketchup and Worcestershire.
5. Glaze. Return prawns. Add slurry gradually and toss only until glossy and coated.
6. Serve. Check seasoning and serve immediately with fried rice or noodles.

Spicy Pepper Masala Sausage

Pork sausage coated in a concentrated garlic-shallot masala with curry leaves, dry and fresh chillies, black pepper, turmeric, garam masala and fennel.

Main250 g pork sausage
StyleDry masala coating
HeatMedium-high
FinishPepper + curry leaves

Ready: everything is cut and within reach.
2

Brown sausage

2 tbsp coconut oil250 g sausage
Heat oil, brown the sausage surfaces, then remove from the pan.
Look for: browned edges.
3

Aromatic base

2 tbsp coconut oil50 g shallots25 g garlic2–3 dry chillies1 sprig curry leaves
Cook together until fragrant.
Watch: do not burn the garlic.
4

Add masala

½ tsp turmeric1 tsp Kashmiri chilli1 tsp black pepper½ tsp garam masala½ tsp fennel
Stir the spices briefly through the aromatic base.
Important: bloom briefly; do not scorch the powders.
5

Make coating

1 tbsp ketchup2–3 fresh chillies½ tsp salt1–2 tbsp water
Mix into the masala. Add only enough water to make a thick coating.
Correct texture: thick and clingy, not runny.
6

Coat and finish

browned sausage½ tsp black pepper1 sprig curry leaves
Return sausage, coat evenly, then add the final pepper and curry leaves.
Final result: glossy dry masala with fresh pepper and curry-leaf aroma.

Ingredients

Pork sausages, sliced lengthwise/quartered250 g
Coconut oil — sausage stage2 tbsp
Coconut oil — masala stage2 tbsp
Garlic, finely chopped25 g / 18–20 cloves
Shallots, thinly sliced50 g / 25–30
Dry red chillies2–3
Curry leaves — base1 sprig
Turmeric powder½ tsp
Kashmiri chilli powder1 tsp
Black pepper — masala1 tsp
Garam masala½ tsp
Fennel powder½ tsp
Ketchup1 tbsp
Fresh green/red chillies2–3
Salt½ tsp, adjust
Water1–2 tbsp
Black pepper — finish½ tsp
Curry leaves — finish1 sprig

Shopping List

Flavour Architecture

Rich cooking base: coconut oil.
Aromatic foundation: a substantial garlic + shallot layer.
Fragrant heat: dry chilli + fresh chilli + two additions of black pepper.
Masala body: turmeric + Kashmiri chilli + garam masala + fennel.
Herbal aroma: curry leaves in the base and again at the finish.
Balance: ketchup contributes sweetness, tang and moisture.
Texture: only 1–2 tbsp water is used so the masala remains a thick coating rather than becoming a sauce.

Cooking Method

1. Mise en place. Slice sausages. Chop garlic, slice shallots and fresh chillies. Measure all dry spices separately.
2. Brown sausage. Heat the first 2 tbsp coconut oil and brown the sausage pieces. Remove and reserve.
3. Build aromatics. Add the second oil portion. Cook shallots, garlic, dry chillies and the first curry leaves until aromatic.
4. Bloom spices. Add turmeric, Kashmiri chilli, 1 tsp black pepper, garam masala and fennel. Cook briefly without scorching.
5. Form coating. Add ketchup, fresh chillies, salt and 1–2 tbsp water. Mix into a thick masala.
6. Coat. Return sausage and toss until the masala adheres evenly.
7. Finish. Add the final ½ tsp freshly crushed black pepper and second sprig of curry leaves for a fresher pepper-and-curry-leaf aroma.

Masala & Spice Interaction

Turmeric + Kashmiri chilli
Earthy foundation + red pepper character. Together they build the colour and base of the masala. Too much turmeric can become earthy or bitter; extra Kashmiri chilli pushes colour and chilli warmth.
Black pepper + fennel
Sharp aromatic heat + sweet fragrance. Fennel rounds the pepper and gives the masala a more perfumed character.
Garam masala + fennel + black pepper
Warm spice complexity + sweet aroma + sharp heat. This combination creates the deeper finished masala character.
Dry chilli + fresh chilli + black pepper
Three layers of heat: dry chilli gives background heat, fresh chilli gives brighter immediate heat, and black pepper gives fragrant sharpness.
Garlic + shallot + curry leaves + masala
The aromatic foundation carries the dry spices. This prevents the dish from tasting like spice powder alone and gives the coating depth.
Ketchup + spice mixture
Sweetness, acidity and moisture soften the aggressive edges of chilli and pepper while helping form the coating.

Change the Masala

More peppery
Increase black pepper slightly. Do not increase chilli powder if pepper aroma is the target.
More red / chilli character
Increase Kashmiri chilli moderately.
More warm masala aroma
Increase garam masala slightly; large increases can dominate the sausage.
More sweet fragrance
Increase fennel slightly.
More chilli heat
Increase fresh or dry chilli rather than garam masala.
Milder masala
Reduce chilli and pepper while retaining the garlic, shallot, curry leaves and basic spice structure.

What changes the taste?

More fennel: sweeter, more distinctly aromatic masala character.
More black pepper: sharper, fragrant heat.
More ketchup: sweeter, tangier and wetter coating.
More water: loosens the masala; too much removes the dry-coated character.

Thai Red Chicken Curry

Chicken with red curry paste, coconut milk, three bell peppers and a savoury-sweet balance of fish sauce and brown sugar, finished with fresh coriander.

Main500 g chicken
Sauce600 ml coconut milk
Curry paste4 tbsp
StyleCreamy red curry

Easy Cook Along

Use only the ingredients shown at each stage.

1

Prepare

500 g chicken strips40 g onion25 g garlic10 g ginger180 g mixed pepperscoriander
DO: Slice chicken and onion, finely chop garlic and ginger, cut all bell peppers into long strips, and separate coriander stems from leaves.
WHY: Preparing everything first keeps the fast curry stages controlled. LOOK FOR: Evenly cut ingredients ready beside the pan. WHAT CHANGES: Uneven cuts cook at different rates.
2

Build the aromatic base

3 tbsp vegetable oil40 g onion25 g garlic10 g ginger
Heat oil and cook onion, garlic and ginger until aromatic.
Goal: fragrant aromatics without burning the garlic.
3

Cook the curry paste

4 tbsp Thai red curry paste
Stir the curry paste through the aromatic base so its spices and chilli become fragrant.
This stage matters: the paste becomes the dominant flavour foundation.
4

Add colour and aroma

60 g yellow pepper60 g green pepper60 g red pepper1 tbsp coriander stems
Add the peppers and chopped coriander stems and combine with the curry base.
5

Build the curry sauce

600 ml coconut cream/milk1 tbsp fish sauce1 tbsp brown sugar½ tsp salt
Add coconut milk, fish sauce, sugar and salt. Stir until the red curry paste is evenly dispersed.
Balance: coconut gives richness, fish sauce gives salty umami, and sugar rounds the chilli and salt.
6

Cook the chicken

500 g chicken strips
Cook the chicken in the curry until fully cooked and the sauce has developed around it.
Food safety: poultry should reach at least 74°C in the centre.
7

Finish

3–4 tbsp coriander leaves
Taste the curry, correct the balance if needed, then finish with chopped coriander leaves.

Coconut cream/milk600 ml

Shopping List

Flavour Architecture

Aromatic foundation: onion + garlic + ginger.
Primary curry character: Thai red curry paste.
Richness: full-fat coconut milk/cream softens and carries the curry paste.
Savoury / salty: fish sauce supplies concentrated umami and salt.
Sweet balance: brown sugar rounds chilli, salt and curry intensity.
Freshness: coriander stems cook into the sauce; leaves are reserved for the fresh finish.
Vegetable character: three bell peppers add sweetness, colour and texture.

Change the Curry

More curry paste: stronger chilli, spice and aromatic concentration.
More coconut milk: milder, creamier and more diluted curry character.
More fish sauce: saltier and more savoury; increase carefully.
More brown sugar: sweeter and softer chilli perception.
More ginger: fresher, warmer aromatic sharpness.

Homemade Red Curry Paste Alternative

2 tbsp chilli powder + 1 tbsp paprika + 1 tsp cumin + 1 tsp ground coriander + 1 tsp garlic powder or 1 minced garlic clove + 1 tsp ginger powder or 1 inch fresh ginger + 1 tbsp soy/fish sauce + 1 tsp lemongrass paste or lime zest + 1 tsp onion powder or 1 small minced shallot. Mix into a paste.
Important: this is a substitute with a similar general profile; it will not exactly reproduce commercial Thai red curry paste.

Fish Sauce Alternatives

Soy sauce or tamari: replaces salty umami, but not the distinct fish-sauce character.
Soy + lime: salty umami with added brightness.
Soy + seaweed: adds a marine note.
Vegan direction: soy sauce + mushroom broth + a small amount of vinegar.

Flavour Architecture

Prawn seasoning: salt + pepper.
Earthy colour: turmeric.

Why This Combination Works

Yogurt + cashew: tangy creaminess plus nutty body creates a marinade that clings to the prawns.
Turmeric + green chilli + coriander: earthy warmth, fresh heat and herbal aroma sit inside that creamy coating.

More green chilli: stronger fresh chilli heat.
More turmeric: deeper yellow and earthier flavour; too much can dominate.
More coriander: fresher herbal character.
Vegetables · Japanese-style Dashi

Japanese Vegetable Dashi Soup

A vegetable soup built on kelp-and-bonito dashi, with garlic, onion, carrot, sweet potato, cabbage and tomato, finished with olive oil.

Stock1 L dashi
Bonito5 g
Sake15 g
StyleLight savoury soup

Easy Cook Along

Every stage now explains what to do, why it matters, what to look for and what changes.

1

Cut vegetables evenly

⅓ carrot⅓ sweet potato½ onioncabbage
DO: Finely chop the vegetables. Put the cut sweet potato in water.
WHY: The source explains that even fine pieces heat more consistently and give a more even texture; soaking is used to prevent sweet-potato discoloration. LOOK FOR: Similar-sized pieces. WHAT CHANGES: Larger or uneven pieces need different cooking times.
2

Peel the tomato

1 tomato
DO: Make cuts in the tomato, briefly blanch in lightly boiling water, then cool in cold water and peel.
WHY: The blanch-and-cool step makes the skin peel cleanly. LOOK FOR: Skin beginning to loosen. WHAT CHANGES: Skipping this leaves the tomato skin in the finished soup.
3

Make dashi

1 L water1 piece kelp5 g bonito flakes
DO: Heat the kelp in 1 L water. When it boils, remove the kelp and add the bonito flakes; the source wraps the bonito in paper towel.
WHY: This creates the soup's savoury stock base. LOOK FOR: Clear infused stock. WHAT CHANGES: This stock is the main umami foundation of the recipe.
4

Build vegetable base

10 g olive oil1 garlic cloveonioncarrotsweet potatosalt + pepper
DO: Add olive oil, then garlic, onion, carrot and sweet potato. Stir-fry lightly and season with salt and pepper.
WHY: This creates an aromatic vegetable foundation before liquid is added. LOOK FOR: Lightly cooked, aromatic vegetables rather than heavy browning. WHAT CHANGES: More browning would produce a deeper roasted character.
5

Add sake and dashi

15 g sakeprepared dashi
DO: Add sake after the light stir-fry, then pour in the dashi.
WHY: Sake contributes aroma and the dashi becomes the main soup liquid. LOOK FOR: Vegetables surrounded by the light savoury stock. WHAT CHANGES: More dashi makes the vegetable flavours more diluted.
6

Simmer remaining vegetables

laurier / bay leafcabbagepeeled tomato
DO: Add laurier, cabbage and tomato and simmer.
WHY: Simmering softens the vegetables and integrates their flavour into the stock. LOOK FOR: Tender vegetables with identifiable pieces. WHAT CHANGES: Longer simmering gives softer vegetables and a more integrated soup.
7

Taste and finish

saltpepperolive oil
DO: Taste, correct salt and pepper if necessary, then finish with olive oil.
WHY: The source uses the final oil for richness and aroma. LOOK FOR: Light savoury broth with balanced seasoning. WHAT CHANGES: More finishing oil gives a richer mouthfeel and stronger olive-oil aroma.
